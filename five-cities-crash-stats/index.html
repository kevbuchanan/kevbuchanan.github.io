Crash records · Arroyo Grande, Grover Beach, Pismo Beach · 2021–2025

2,543 crashes in the Five Cities

Over five years the three cities recorded 2,543 crashes. 888 people were hurt and 10 were killed. 146 of those crashes, one in eighteen, involved someone on a bike or on foot. Not one involved an electric bike.

No bicyclist or pedestrian involved Bicycle On foot

One crash in eighteen, one injury in seven, one death in two. This is not an argument that bikes are dangerous. It is the opposite. Four of those five deaths were people on foot struck by a driver, and the fifth was a cyclist who hit a fixed object with no vehicle involved. A person outside a car has no bumper and no airbag, so the same collision that costs two drivers an insurance claim costs a pedestrian their life. The danger in both halves of this page is the same: motor vehicles, driven by people, at speed.

Why Oceano and the Dunes are not hereOceano is unincorporated, so the state files its crashes under the county rather than as a city and they cannot be cleanly separated. A rough estimate by map coordinates gives 67 crashes and 17 injuries in 2025, one of them a bicycle. Separately, the Oceano Dunes state park district recorded 142 crashes with 153 people injured and 2 killed in 2025, more injuries than Grover Beach and Oceano put together. Those are off-road vehicle crashes in the recreation area, a different problem with different answers, and they are excluded from every figure here.

One gap worth knowing aboutGrover Beach police filed 54 crash reports in 2023 and 57 in 2024, against roughly 140 in each of 2021, 2022, and 2025. Nothing in the surrounding county moved that way, and that department is the only agency filing for the city. Those two years are a gap in the paperwork rather than two safer years, and the five-year totals on this page are short by whatever went unrecorded.
